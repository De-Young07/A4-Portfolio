About Me

I build for the people
everyone else ignores.

"I thought I understood the problem — so I started building. I hit a dead end. That dead end became the real beginning."

I'm a final-year B.Tech Statistics student (GPA 4.30/5.00) at the Federal University of Technology Minna, simultaneously completing a Data Science degree at WorldQuant University. My work sits at the intersection of statistical rigour and real-world deployment — built specifically for the constraints most engineers design around: patchy connectivity, low-resource hardware, and users who can't afford a learning curve.

I came into data science with a single question: why does so much perishable food move across Nigeria and rot before it reaches people who need it? I built what I thought was a solution. Then I hit the walls that matter — the market doesn't behave the way you modelled, traders don't trust an app built by someone who's never traded, and the data you assumed existed simply doesn't.

That failure became TradeFlow NG — a fully deployed agricultural trade optimization platform now active across 8 Nigerian states. Not rebuilt from scratch. Rebuilt from experience.

I serve as Director of Research at ASSN FUTMinna and Tech Lead at DSN FUTMinna. Through my consulting practice, A4 Analytics, I build bespoke systems for businesses — pharmacies, distributors, and organisations that need production-grade tools without enterprise budgets. In June 2026, my team placed 1st at the HealthTrace Hackathon — a disease surveillance system built in three days. Every project starts with the same question: what does the person at the end of this system actually need? Not what's technically impressive. What solves the problem.

✦ Recent Build · June 2026
Lafiya — Low-Connectivity Disease Surveillance Backend

Contact-tracing systems assume constant internet connectivity — an assumption that fails across much of Nigeria, where field devices regularly lose signal for hours or days. Built in 3 days with a 3-person team, I owned the backend and data engineering layer: the IngestService (schema validation, MAC-address privacy hashing, deduplication) and the RelayService (encrypted store-and-forward sync through intermediary relay phones, server-only decryption), so surveillance data reaches the intelligence pipeline whether the source device is online or offline — and relay phones never see the health payloads they carry. The SQLite-based storage layer I built feeds five downstream intelligence engines: exposure risk scoring, vitals anomaly detection, contact network mapping, outbreak zone clustering, and device health monitoring.

Philosophy
"The best technology starts with a deep understanding of the conditions it has to survive — not the conditions it was designed under."
01
Build for the conditions, not the demo.
A system that works perfectly with stable internet and high-end hardware tells you nothing about whether it'll work in the field. I design for the worst realistic case first.
02
Build systems, not experiments.
A 92% accuracy model that lives in a notebook helps nobody. I build pipelines that deploy, persist, monitor, and work in real environments — not ideal ones.
03
Dead ends are data.
TradeFlow NG was rebuilt after hitting every wall that matters. That restart — from experience, not from scratch — taught me more than any course.
04
Let the work speak before I do.
I'd rather show a working system, a judged win, or a deployed pipeline than describe myself as skilled. Specificity and proof build trust faster than any pitch.
